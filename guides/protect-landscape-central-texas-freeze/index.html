How to Protect Your Landscape from a Central Texas Freeze

Central Texas freezes are rare, sudden and hard on plants. A little preparation before the front arrives saves a lot of heartbreak after.

Home/Guides/How to Protect Your Landscape from a...

Most of the year our climate is mild, which is exactly why a hard freeze does so much damage — our landscapes aren't built for it, and the cold often arrives fast. A few steps before the front hits make the difference between a scare and a loss.

Before the freeze

  • Water deeply a day or two ahead. Moist soil holds heat better than dry soil and insulates roots.
  • Mulch beds. A few inches of mulch protects root zones — the part of the plant most worth saving.
  • Cover tender plants with frost cloth or fabric (not plastic touching the leaves), draped to the ground to trap ground warmth.
  • Protect irrigation. Insulate exposed backflow preventers and shut off/drain vulnerable lines.

After the freeze — wait before you cut

The hardest advice to follow: resist pruning right away. Damaged growth, even if it looks bad, protects the living tissue beneath it from the next cold snap. Wait until the danger of frost has passed in spring, then cut back to healthy growth. Cutting too early often kills a plant that would have recovered.
