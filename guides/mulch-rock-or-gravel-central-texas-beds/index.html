Mulch, Rock or Gravel: Which Is Right for Your Central Texas Beds?

Mulch, rock and decomposed granite each have a job. Choosing right saves water, cuts weeds, and keeps beds looking sharp for years.

Home/Guides/Mulch, Rock or Gravel: Which Is Right...

The material you put on top of a bed does more work than people realize — it controls water loss, weeds, soil temperature and the whole look of your landscape. Here's how the three main options compare for Central Texas.

Mulch — best for planting beds

Hardwood mulch is the top choice around plants. It holds moisture, keeps roots cool, suppresses weeds and slowly feeds the soil as it breaks down. The trade-off is that it decomposes, so it needs a refresh every year or two. For flower beds and around trees and shrubs, it's usually the right call.

Decomposed granite — best for paths and low-water areas

"DG" gives you a firm, natural-looking surface for paths, seating areas and xeriscape zones. It doesn't break down like mulch and pairs beautifully with native plantings, but it isn't ideal right up against thirsty plants that want a richer, moisture-holding cover.

Rock & gravel — best for drainage and permanence

River rock and gravel shine in drainage features, dry creek beds and high-traffic or fire-wise zones where you want something that never needs replacing. The catch: rock radiates heat and doesn't feed the soil, so a yard buried in rock can bake — use it with intention, not everywhere.

The usual answer: a mix

Most well-designed Central Texas yards use all three — mulch in the planting beds, DG on the paths, rock where water moves. We help you put each in the right place. See mulch, rock & gravel services or ask for a recommendation.
