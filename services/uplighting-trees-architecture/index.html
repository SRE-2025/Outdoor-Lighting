Uplighting for Trees & Architecture

Uplighting is what gives a property that magazine look at night. We place directional LED fixtures at the base of your signature oaks, stonework and architecture and aim them to graze texture and catch the canopy — creating depth, shadow and drama that makes the whole yard feel intentional after dark.

What's included

  • Uplighting for feature trees and canopies
  • Grazing light that shows off stone and texture
  • Balanced brightness and beam angles
  • Highlights architecture, columns and facades
  • Warm color temperature that flatters the home

The technique behind the magazine look

Uplighting works by placing a directional fixture low and aiming it up a surface — the trunk and canopy of a tree, a stone chimney, a column, a facade. That upward angle is what creates drama: it grazes texture so bark and stone come alive, throws long shadows for depth, and lifts light into a canopy so a tree glows from within after dark.

The art is in restraint and aim. Too many fixtures or the wrong beam angle turns drama into a wall of glare. We balance brightness, beam spread, and placement so each feature reads clearly and the property as a whole feels layered rather than washed flat.

Made for Hill Country trees and stonework

Central Texas hands us some of the best uplighting subjects anywhere — mature live oaks with sprawling canopies, limestone facades, and the stone and masonry that show up on so many local homes. A big live oak uplit from a few angles becomes the centerpiece of the entire yard.

On the larger lots common in Dripping Springs, Driftwood, Wimberley, and Bee Cave, uplighting is often the difference between a property that vanishes into the dark and one that feels finished and grand at night. It gives all that acreage and architecture something to do after sunset.

Warm, glare-controlled, and built to last

We use a warm color temperature that flatters natural bark, stone, and stucco rather than the cold blue cast that makes a home look industrial. Fixtures are shielded and aimed to keep glare out of windows and eyes, and we account for how a tree will grow so the aim holds up over time.

All of it is low-voltage LED, weather-rated for our climate, with concealed wiring and a dusk schedule. It is backed by our 1-year labor warranty and quick service from our shop in Buda.

Common questions

Uplighting for Trees & Architecture FAQs

Will uplighting hurt my trees?

No. We use low-voltage LED fixtures that run cool and place them at the base of the tree, not in it. There is no heat or wiring that harms the tree, and we plan placement with the tree's growth in mind so the look holds up over the years.

How many fixtures does a big live oak need?

A large live oak usually looks best lit from two or three angles so the canopy and trunk read with depth rather than a single flat wash. The exact number depends on the tree's size and shape, which we assess during the free consultation.

Can you uplight my home's stone or facade too?

Absolutely. Grazing light up a limestone facade, a stone chimney, or columns is one of the most dramatic things uplighting does. We often combine tree and architectural uplighting so the whole property reads as one intentional composition at night.

Why warm light instead of bright white or blue?

Warm light flatters natural materials — bark, stone, and stucco all look richer under it, while cold blue-white tends to make a home look harsh and industrial. We choose a warm color temperature specifically to make your features look their best.

Will the fixtures glare into my windows or the street?

No. We shield and aim each fixture to put light on the feature and keep it out of eyes, windows, and your neighbors' view. Controlling glare is a core part of making uplighting look professional rather than harsh.
