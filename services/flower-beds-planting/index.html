Flower Beds & Planting

The right plant in the right place barely needs you. We design and install flower beds and borders with native and adapted plants suited to Central Texas — color that comes back, structure year-round, and far less water than a typical bed.

What's included

  • Native & drought-tolerant plant selection
  • New bed installs and full garden makeovers
  • Seasonal color and perennial borders
  • Bed shaping, edging and soil amendment
  • Pollinator- and pet-friendly options

The right plant in the right place barely needs you

Most struggling beds in Central Texas aren't neglected, they're just planted wrong: sun-lovers in shade, thirsty annuals in a hot, dry strip, or nursery plants that were never suited to our soil and heat to begin with. Get the plant, the light, and the soil matched up and a bed practically takes care of itself.

That's our whole approach to planting. We start from what will actually thrive in your specific spot, then design the color, texture, and structure around it, instead of forcing a look that fights the conditions all year.

Native and adapted, built for our climate

We lean hard on native and well-adapted plants because they earn it. Salvias, lantana, esperanza, Texas sage, autumn sage, rock rose, and ornamental grasses shrug off our heat, handle the watering restrictions, and come back year after year. Mix in perennials for structure and a bit of seasonal color and you get a bed that looks lush without a garden hose running constantly.

Native doesn't mean sparse. It means color that returns on its own, blooms that feed pollinators, and a bed that still looks alive in a dry August when the thirsty stuff has given up.

Beds that start below the surface

A good bed is built, not just planted. We shape and edge the bed cleanly, amend our stubborn clay or thin caliche so roots can actually spread, set plants at the right depth and spacing for how they'll grow in, and finish with mulch to hold moisture and keep the soil cool. That prep is why our beds fill in and last instead of stalling out.

We also plan for maturity. Nothing looks worse in three years than plants crammed together because they looked small at install. We space for the plant you'll have, not the one in the pot.

Makeovers and pollinator- or pet-friendly options

Whether it's a fresh border along the walk or a full front-yard garden makeover, we design for how the bed will read from the street and from inside the house. Want it to draw bees and butterflies, or to stay safe for a dog that treats the yard like a racetrack? We'll build the plant list around that.

The result is a bed that blooms without a fight and looks intentional in every season.

Common questions

Flower Beds & Planting FAQs

What flowers actually survive a Central Texas summer?

Tough, sun-loving natives and adapted perennials like salvia, lantana, esperanza, Texas sage, and rock rose handle our heat and come back year after year. We build beds around plants proven to thrive in your specific light and soil, so you're not replanting every time it turns hot.

When is the best time to plant new beds?

Fall is ideal because plants get to establish roots over our mild winter before facing summer heat, and spring is a strong second. We can plant in summer, but it takes more careful watering to carry new plants through the heat, and we'll tell you what the season asks of you.

Are native plants boring or sparse-looking?

Not at all. Native and adapted plantings can be just as lush and colorful as a traditional bed, they just get there with plants that belong here. You get blooms that feed pollinators, structure year-round, and far less watering than a bed full of thirsty annuals.

Can you make my beds pet- and pollinator-friendly?

Yes. We regularly design beds around drawing bees and butterflies, keeping plants safe for dogs, or both. Tell us how the yard gets used and we'll build the plant list to match.

Do you refresh existing beds or only build new ones?

Both. A lot of our planting work is reviving tired beds, reshaping and re-edging them, amending the soil, pulling what's failing, and replanting with things that will actually last. Sometimes a makeover of what you have beats starting over.
